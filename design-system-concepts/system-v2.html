v2Design language — from scratch

Read the ground,
not just the figure.

Stripe's motif is literal — it draws stripes. Mine should be just as literal to my idea: that every problem is a figure standing on a ground, and the ground is what decides the answer. This document builds a language from that one idea, bounded by one scarcity — attention — and proposes two visual directions. Same author, two voices: one that sells, one that explains.

00The idea

Figure & ground, as a design system.

Gestalt: you can't see a figure without a ground. Signal processing: there's no signal without a carrier and a noise floor. TRIZ: a problem only makes sense inside the system that produced it. Same truth, three vocabularies — and the thing I actually do: I read the ground, separate signal from noise, and find the function a problem is quietly serving.

So the language shouldn't decorate. It should perform figure/ground: a quiet ground always present, one sharp figure on top, and — when you lean in — the ground becomes readable (the system, the annotations, the why).

01The constraints

Scarcity is the brief.

Constraints made the old masters: no blue paint, so they invented with ochre. In digital the scarce resource isn't pigment — it's attention. Build the whole system to honor that, and three rules fall out on their own.

RULE 01 · ATTENTION

One idea per view

Exactly one figure is bright at a time. If everything shouts, nothing is heard. Every screen answers one question; the rest is quiet ground, one scroll away.

RULE 02 · ONE PAINT

Monochrome + a single signal

Ink and paper, plus one accent — the “signal” color, spent only on the thing that matters. Color is scarce on purpose, so it always means something.

RULE 03 · THE HUMAN MARK

One detail made by hand

Vibe-coded pages all look the same. Each page earns one deliberate, hand-tuned detail — an annotation, an irregular trace, a real footnote — proof a person was here.

02The unifying mechanism

Two voices, one dial.

Business and engineering pages aren't two systems — they're two settings of the same one. Drag the dial. Left, the figure dominates: big, glanceable, sells. Right, the ground is exposed: grid, axes, function-labels, the schematic underneath. Same content, same author — the knob is just how much ground you show.

03Direction

A — “Signal” / the instrument

Built on stripe.dev's darker, technical foundation. The ground is a near-black instrument panel; the figure is a single trace of electric cyan — the signal. The unifying motif is the baseline: a horizontal axis that runs through every page. Business pages show one clean peak; engineering pages turn the whole thing into an oscilloscope.

04Direction

B — “Ground” / the field

Built on stripe.com's lighter, editorial warmth — but on a dev grid. The ground is a quiet dot-field; the figure is sharp ink with one vermilion mark. 05Read & decide

Same spine, two temperatures.

Both directions are one system on a dial, honor the one-idea / one-paint / one-hand-mark constraints, and carry the figure/ground idea literally. They differ in temperature and risk.

A · Signal — the instrument

Dark, electric, unmistakably “engineer”

  • Most distinctive — reads as a lab instrument, not a SaaS site
  • The baseline/waveform motif is genuinely ownable
  • Engineering pages are the native habitat
  • Dark + neon is harder to keep readable when selling
  • Business audience may find it cold
B · Ground — the field

Light, editorial, warm, hireable

  • Sells effortlessly; reads senior and human
  • The margin-of-function-labels is a fresh, ownable device
  • Easiest to keep readable across long pages
  • Lighter touch — must guard against “just clean”
  • Needs the hand-mark discipline to not feel generic

My recommendation: B “Ground” as the home skin (it sells you and reads senior), and treat A “Signal” as a mode the dial can reach on deep engineering pages — the oscilloscope is the far-right end of the same knob. One language, figure on the left, ground on the right.

06Next steps
  1. Pick a temperature — A, B, or “B-home + A-mode” — and confirm the single signal color (cyan vs vermilion, or another).
  2. I write the tokens + primitives as one stylesheet: the ground field, the figure type scale, the one-accent rule, the function-label component, the dial states.
  3. Prove it on two pages first — the self-presentation (business) and the skill (engineering) — so we see both ends of the dial live before rolling out.
  4. Then convert the rest and retire the four mismatched styles.
