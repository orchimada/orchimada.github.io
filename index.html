02Approach

The job isn't to build faster.
It's to decide what not to build — faster.

"Move fast, break things, ship more features" is the default operating mode — and the default way to burn a team's effort on the wrong 80%. I work from the other end: structural analysis to find the 20% of moves that produce 80% of the impact, and a clear, defensible no to everything else.

/01

Judgment over velocity

AI made prototypes cheap — judgment is the scarce asset now. I frame opportunities in commercial terms, validate demand before building, and lead trade-off conversations in business outcomes, not delivery dates.

/02

Excel where it works, big guns where it counts

Most product problems are discipline problems wearing tooling costumes. Four of six core CPO challenges are solved by a spreadsheet plus an operating rhythm — decision rights, cadence, ownership. I save the heavy machinery for the two that earn it: hands-on AI builds and insight synthesis at scale.

/03

Figure from the background

I judge products by the behavior they install, not the features they list. Surfacing invisible assumptions, working the seams between domains, naming what becomes scarce when your technology makes something abundant — that keeps the product vision alive and competitors a category behind.

/04

Product sense, backed by evidence

Probes, not opinions. I hunt the unnamed pain — expensive workarounds, apologies, nostalgia for the old way — then run every idea through an adversarial critique loop. Killed ideas get epitaphs; survivors get the cheapest test that could prove them wrong.

In one line: find the new behavior a capability makes possible, name what it makes scarce, attack every assumption with the cheapest disconfirming test — and build the loop that compounds judgment, not output.
