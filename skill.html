Judgment over Velocity

A structured product-judgment engine for deciding what not to build — faster. My working methodology — the spreadsheet test, the 80/20 cut, figure/ground analysis, and adversarial evidence probes — wrapped into a skill Claude runs on your own roadmap.

What it does

Give Claude a product idea, a backlog, a "should we buy a tool?" question, or any CPO-level problem — and instead of cheerfully helping you build it, the skill classifies the problem first and runs it through four kill-gates. Ideas that die get epitaphs (what killed them, what would resurrect them). Ideas that survive get the cheapest disconfirming test — not the cheapest test that could confirm them.

It is explicitly willing to answer "don't build anything" — the most common right answer and the rarest one product teams hear.

The four gates
1

The spreadsheet test

Most product problems are discipline problems wearing tooling costumes. Could a competent operator solve this with a table, an operating rhythm, and clear decision rights? Field calibration: 4 of 6 core CPO problem clusters fail this gate. Only experiential gaps (you must build to learn) and synthesis-at-scale earn heavy machinery.

2

The 80/20 structural cut

Every option framed in commercial terms — what revenue, retention, cost, or risk number does it move? The field is scored as a whole, not debated item by item. The no-list is the deliverable: each cut gets a one-line reason a stakeholder could repeat.

3

Figure/ground analysis

Products judged by the behavior they install, not the features they list. What becomes scarce when your capability makes something abundant? Which assumptions are load-bearing — and which are actively melting? Does the idea sit in a defensible seam between domains, or in the middle of an incumbent's home turf?

4

Evidence probes & adversarial loop

Probes, not opinions. Demand signals ranked by strength: expensive workarounds > apologies > nostalgia > stated feature requests. Every load-bearing assumption gets attacked; the survivor's prize is a disconfirming test with the kill-condition stated in advance.

Each gate can kill the idea — killing early is the point. An idea that dies at Gate 1 saves everything downstream.

Every run ends in a verdict
## Verdict

**Classification:** DISCIPLINE | TOOLING-EARNED | HYBRID
**The 20%:** the 1–3 moves that carry the impact, each with its business number
**The no-list:** everything cut, one defensible line each
**Behavior installed / scarcity owned:** one line
**Riskiest assumption:** the load-bearing assumption most likely to be false
**Cheapest disconfirming test:** what to run, what it costs, what result kills it
**Epitaphs:** killed ideas — what killed each, what would resurrect it

Installation

⌨️ Claude Code (CLI)

  1. Download the .skill file — it's a regular zip archive.
  2. Unzip it into your personal skills directory: 
    # personal — available in every project
unzip judgment-over-velocity.skill -d ~/.claude/skills/

# or project-level — shared with your team via git
unzip judgment-over-velocity.skill -d .claude/skills/
  3. Restart Claude Code (or start a new session). Verify with /skills or just ask: "do you have the judgment-over-velocity skill?"

💬 claude.ai (web & desktop)

  1. Download the .skill file.
  2. Open Settings → Capabilities → Skills and upload the file. (Skills require a paid plan; if you don't see the section, enable code execution / skills in feature previews.)
  3. The skill activates automatically whenever you ask Claude to evaluate an idea, prioritize a backlog, or judge a build-vs-buy question.

"We're thinking of building an internal dashboard to track product KPIs across teams. Should we?"

Where this comes from

This is the operating method behind my approach — built over nine years of personalization and growth work at Sber and Magnit OMNI, where the difference between a 10× experiment and a wasted quarter was almost never execution speed. It was what got cut before anyone wrote code.
