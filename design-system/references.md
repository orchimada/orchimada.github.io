# References

What each source contributed to AD·OS.

## Stripe — product (stripe.com)
The business voice. Light surfaces, enormous whitespace, big confident type, one
accent, one clear CTA per block. A literal brand motif (the gradient stripe).
**Taken:** the "run it bright to sell" half, and the discipline of a single
accent plus a single call to action.

## Stripe — developer (stripe.dev)
The engineering voice. Dark, technical, code-forward, monospace, grid energy.
**Taken:** the "run it dark to explain" half, and the lesson that one brand can
hold both rooms with one type system and one accent.

## Nothing (nothing.tech)
Confidence through restraint. True monochrome, a single red accent, dot-matrix
display type, exposed internals, big and calm. Spec sheets made beautiful.
**Taken:** the dot field as the figure/ground motif, the one-paint red, and the
proof that the technical room can be the boldest, not the most cramped.

## Teenage Engineering (teenage.engineering)
The website as a physical instrument: labeled jacks, segment displays, buttons
with status LEDs, knobs, silkscreen function labels grouped by purpose.
**Taken:** the instrument metaphor, the MODE switch that turns abstract
"temperature" into a real control, and the parts-bin vocabulary.

## The synthesis
- Stripe gives the **structure** (one brand, two temperatures, one accent).
- Nothing gives the **motif and the nerve** (dot field, one red, bold-not-busy).
- Teenage Engineering gives the **physicality** (operate the abstraction).
- The figure/ground idea is the **spine** that makes all three feel personal
  rather than borrowed.
